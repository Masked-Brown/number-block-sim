## What the leak was worth: almost exactly nothing

Audit 0019's real find was that `expectimax-d2-v1` and `expectimax-d3-v1` read the engine's
real, unknowable next block at their search leaves, through `next-merge-ready`. The audit
measured the DECISION effect on 60 paired train games (7.8 per cent of depth-2 moves flip when
the leaked feature is zeroed) and left the score effect properly unmeasured. Both leak-free
versions have now sat the whole exam.

| depth | leaked row (v1) | leak-free row (v2) | median ratio, paired | 95% CI | win rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 2 | 427,986 | 428,990 | 1.00x | 0.89 to 1.09 | 242/500, 44.0% to 52.8% |
| 3 | 643,996 | 634,826 | 0.99x | 0.92 to 1.07 | 255/500, 46.6% to 55.4% |

**The leak changed a lot of decisions and no results.** Both paired difference intervals contain
zero and both win-rate intervals contain 50 per cent, on all 500 shared seeds. At depth 2 the
honest agent is a hair ahead; at depth 3 a hair behind. Neither difference would survive a
different exam, and the direction is not even consistent between the two depths.

That is the strongest possible outcome for the experiment's conclusions and the weakest possible
excuse for the code: the ladder's depth findings never depended on the leak, and the leak was
still wrong, because an agent that reads an unknowable draw is not measuring what its row claims
to measure. Both readings now stand on the record: the v1 rows stay in the ladder, marked
superseded, because the PAIR is the measurement above.

One detail worth keeping. The leak-free depth-3 row has a lower median but a fatter right tail:
mean 771,531 against 739,312, max 4,062,920 against 2,710,108, and its longest game ran 1,277
blocks against 1,094. Integrating over the distribution instead of peeking at one draw appears
to trade a little median for a lot of upside, which is what you would expect of a policy that
stops planning around a specific tile it cannot actually know is coming. n is one exam, so this
is an observation and not a finding.

## The fixed-horizon panel, and the thing it exposes

The ladder compares end-of-game scores, and score compounds with survival (RULES.md 5), so a
better agent's advantage is partly that it lives longer. At a fixed horizon everybody gets the
same number of blocks. Median score at N blocks placed, with the share of games still alive:

| agent | 128 | 256 | 384 | 512 | end of game | alive at 256 | alive at 512 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| expectimax-d3-v2 | 34,030 | 133,302 | 277,228 | 469,246 | 634,826 | 100.0% | 80.8% |
| expectimax-d2-v2 | 31,612 | 119,800 | 251,972 | 376,978 | 428,990 | 99.8% | 55.6% |
| heuristic-v2 | 25,072 | 97,648 | 185,660 | 204,618 | 204,618 | 96.4% | 15.8% |
| heuristic-v1 | 23,488 | 87,222 | 141,098 | 141,424 | 141,424 | 85.2% | 6.6% |
| heuristic-v0 | 23,238 | 80,418 | 114,210 | 114,210 | 114,210 | 76.4% | 2.8% |
| greedy-v1 | 17,910 | 35,386 | 35,386 | 35,386 | 35,386 | 14.6% | 0.0% |

The same rungs, read at a horizon instead of at the end:

| rung | 128 | 256 | 384 | 512 | end of game |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| expectimax-d2-v2 over heuristic-v2 | 1.26x | 1.23x | 1.36x | 1.84x | 2.10x |
| expectimax-d3-v2 over expectimax-d2-v2 | 1.08x | 1.11x | 1.10x | 1.24x | 1.48x |
| expectimax-d3-v2 over heuristic-v0 | 1.46x | 1.66x | 2.43x | 4.11x | 5.56x |

**Most of the ladder's spread is survival, not scoring rate.** The champion outscores the
inherited flat agent 5.56x by the end of a game and only 1.66x over their first 256 blocks. Read
at 128 blocks, one ply of lookahead is worth 1.26x rather than 2.10x. The ordering is unchanged
at every horizon, so the ladder is not an artefact, and that was the question the panel was
built to answer. But the SIZE of every gap is mostly a statement about how long each agent
survives, which compounds score under the drifting curve, rather than about how fast it scores
while alive.

This matters for the write-up in two ways. It is the honest reading of what depth buys: search
does not mainly score faster, it mainly dies later. And it says the human-relevant comparison is
the 256-block column, because that is the length of the one practised human game on record.

**On which: AB's 121,496 in 256 blocks sits between the leak-free depth-2 champion's
256-block median of 119,800 and depth 3's 133,302.** Over the blocks they actually played, a
practised human scored at the searching agents' rate, comfortably inside depth 2's own
interquartile range for that horizon (99,708 to 150,916). What they did not do is survive:
99.8 per cent of depth-2 games were still alive at 256 blocks and that game was over. This is
one game by one player and can bear only that much weight, but it is a far more informative
comparison than the end-of-game medians F004 was written against, and F004 has been amended to
say so.

## Instrument notes

- The bootstrap is the plain percentile method over seeds, 10,000 resamples, rng seed 20260805,
  reproducible from the manifest. Paired statistics resample SEED INDICES so both agents are
  always compared on the same games; that is why a 1.48x rung has an interval of 1.37 to 1.65
  rather than the much wider one two independent medians would give.
- Depth-3 cost: 56.3 minutes at six workers for the leak-free row against 37.2 for the leaked
  one, a 1.5x slowdown. Some of that is the leaf expectation (measured 1.22x per move on train
  seeds) and some is contention: a browser and two test suites were running on this machine for
  part of the run. Timing discipline says single runs lie, so treat 1.2 to 1.5x as the range and
  do not quote either end as the cost of honesty.
- The leak-free leaf integrates `next-merge-ready` over the exact live distribution, never a
  truncation. Depth 3's `coverage: 0.9` still truncates its THIRD-block expectation, unchanged
  from v1, so the two depth-3 rows differ by the leak fix alone.
- Both v1 versions were re-verified after the code change that made their leaf mode explicit:
  60 recorded depth-2 games and 8 recorded depth-3 games re-chosen move for move from the
  registered modules, zero mismatches on moves, score or final hash. The v1 rows in `ladder.md`
  are therefore the same measurements the campaign recorded, not a re-run.
- Provenance footnote. The two new eval runs record `workingTreeDirty` differently:
  `false` for the depth-3 run, which launched before this job edited `docs/js/config.js`, and
  `true` for the depth-2 run, which launched after. The edit adds the game v1.2 `performance`
  and `daily` tunable blocks; the eight spawn parameters are byte-identical to HEAD (checked),
  the worker pool is created before the first game and receives its spawn parameters explicitly
  through `workerData`, and both manifests record the parameters actually used. Nothing either
  run computed differs from the committed engine and tuning.

## Findings for the register

Not drafted here as new findings. This run's job amended three existing entries in place, which
the work order authorised explicitly (F001 and F003 mechanism corrections, F004 softened to what
n=1 supports), and appended the matching dated correction to `output/DECISION_LOG.md`. The
amendments are append-only inside their fields, per the work order, so the original wording
remains legible beside the correction.

Two candidate findings are NOT promoted and are recorded here for a later AB-gated pass:

1. **Most of the depth ladder's spread is survival, not scoring rate.** Evidence: the
   fixed-horizon panel above. Would enter as its own finding, or as an amendment to F001's
   mechanism. Falsifier: a horizon panel on a retuned spawn curve showing the gaps hold at 128
   and 256 blocks.
2. **The leaf-preview leak changed 7.8 per cent of decisions and no measurable score.**
   Evidence: the paired table above plus audit 0019's decision-flip measurement. Would enter as
   a methodological finding about how little a plausible-sounding information leak was worth
   here, which is a genuinely useful thing for the write-up to say about its own rigour.
